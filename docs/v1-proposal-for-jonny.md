# Meditate With Me — Version One

**A proposal for the first working website**
Prepared for Jonny Knowles · August 2026

This is a starting point, not a finished decision. I've read your brief properly and I think the heart of it is lovely — the idea that somewhere in the world, right now, someone is sitting with you. What follows is my suggestion for the smallest version of that idea we can put in front of real people quickly.

## What version one is

Someone hears about the site and visits it. They see a simple, quiet page telling them how many minutes remain until the next session begins, and how many people around the world are already waiting for it.

At the top of the hour, a candle lights. A gentle note tells them how many people lit it at the same moment they did.

Before they begin, they choose how long they'd like to sit — anywhere from one to forty-five minutes — and what they'd like to hear underneath. Rain, wind, a distant waterfall, whichever combination suits them, each one adjustable so they can build the exact atmosphere they want. When their time is up, a bell sounds.

That's the whole experience. One page, no instructions needed, working the same way at four in the morning as at four in the afternoon.

---

## The shape of a visit

It's worth walking through it as someone new would experience it, because the details are where this either feels right or doesn't.

**Arriving.** The page is quiet. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing to dismiss. It says how long until the next session, and how many people are already waiting. If a session is already running, they can join it partway through instead of waiting.

**Beginning.** One button. Pressing it lights the candle and starts the sound — a single deliberate gesture rather than something that starts playing at them unannounced.

**Settling.** They can adjust anything without leaving the page: how long they're sitting for, which sounds they want and how loud each one is, which closing bell, which image to rest their eyes on. Everything is remembered for their next visit.

**Sitting.** The screen holds the candle and very little else. The count of others sitting alongside them is there, and can be hidden by anyone who finds a number distracting.

**Ending.** Their bell sounds. Then a moment of quiet before anything at all appears on screen — no immediate ask, no interruption. What comes after that is worth deciding together; my instinct is a single line acknowledging the sit, and nothing more.

## What's included

**The session**

- A new session beginning at the top of every hour, continuously, all day and night
- A shared clock so everyone worldwide joins the same session at the same moment, shown to each person in their own local time
- A countdown to the next session, and the option to join one already in progress
- A candle to rest the eyes on, with two other options for people who prefer something else — we'll choose these together

**The practice**

- A personal timer from one to forty-five minutes, set by each person independently of the session
- A library of ambient sounds that can be layered and mixed, each with its own volume
- A choice of closing bells — a singing bowl, a gong, a struck bell
- A live count of how many people are meditating alongside them, which they can hide

**How people get in**

- No account needed to sit and meditate — people can simply arrive and begin
- An optional account for anyone who wants their preferences remembered across devices
- Deliberately low friction: asking people to register before they've experienced anything is the fastest way to lose them

**Everything around it**

- Works properly on phones, which is where most people will find it
- Usable by people relying on screen readers, and by anyone navigating without a mouse
- Readable in bright light and comfortable in the dark, since a fair number of visits will be late at night
- Privacy notice, terms of use, and an age policy
- Set up so the site appears properly when someone shares it or finds it through a search

## What we're leaving until later

None of this is being dropped. It's being sequenced — built in the order that lets us learn something before we spend on it.

**The live Candle Lighter.** This is the biggest one, and I want to be straightforward about why. A live camera feed running every hour of every day, to an unlimited audience, carries a real and ongoing cost that grows the more successful the site becomes — potentially many thousands of pounds a year, on a site that's free to use. It also brings legal responsibilities around broadcasting volunteers to an anonymous worldwide audience, particularly where younger people might be watching, which need proper attention and probably a solicitor.

There's a practical reason too. Filling every hour means finding, interviewing and approving enough volunteers to cover 8,760 sessions a year, including the ones at four in the morning. That's a large undertaking on its own, quite separate from the website.

Version one delivers the feeling you described — you are not meditating alone — without any of that. If people show up and keep coming back, we'll know the live version is worth building, and we'll have evidence to raise money for it.

**Dharma Circles and the Waiting Room.** A genuinely good idea, and effectively a small social network with its own moderation questions. It deserves its own proper phase rather than being squeezed in.

**Distant Blessings Now.** The most sensitive feature in your brief. Showing a photograph or video of someone who is unwell means handling private medical information about a person who may not be able to consent themselves. I'd want a solicitor's view before we build it.

**Donations.** Straightforward to add — perhaps a day's work — but it needs a registered organisation behind it first. Once that exists, we can switch it on.

**Other languages, calendar reminders, social sharing, the mobile app, streaks.** All sensible, all much easier once the site exists and we can see how people actually use it.

## What I'll need from you

A few things I can't source myself. The first three are the ones that would actually hold work up.

1. **Access to the domain.** You mentioned you've owned it for around twenty years — that's genuinely valuable and worth protecting. I'd like to check together that automatic renewal is switched on and the account is secure. A domain that old lapsing by accident would be a real loss.

2. **The sounds.** This matters more than anything else on the list. Recordings that hold up on good headphones — a singing bowl with real resonance, a proper Tibetan bell — cost money and need buying or recording. Everything else I can build; this I can't. Worth sorting early rather than late.

3. **The visuals.** Three looping images or short films for people to rest their eyes on. I'd suggest a candle plus two others, chosen by you.

4. **Who is legally behind the site.** Whether that's a registered foundation, a charity, or currently just you. I need this to write the privacy notice honestly, and it's what stands between us and accepting donations.

5. **A decision on minimum age.** My recommendation is eighteen and over for now. It's easily relaxed later once the safeguarding side is properly thought through, whereas the reverse is much harder.

6. **Any images of mandalas, yantras or Buddha figures** you'd like used, and ideally someone who can write a sentence about each. I'd rather these carry a little context than sit there as decoration.

## What it costs to run

Close to nothing — and this is part of why version one is shaped the way it is.

The tools I'll build on are free at the scale we'll be starting at, and you already own the domain. Realistically you'd be looking at no running costs at all for the first several months, and modest ones only once the site is genuinely busy.

The exception is the sound and image files, which are a one-off purchase rather than an ongoing cost. Those are worth buying properly — they're the difference between something people return to and something they try once.
