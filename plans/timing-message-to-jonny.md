**Subject:** The 55 minutes — and what we're actually building toward

Hi Jonny,

Your 55/5 change is done and it's on the test site. The slider now stops at 55 minutes, and *until the bell* — the option where you finish at the same moment as everyone else who chose it — now rings at :55 rather than on the hour. So every hour has five clear minutes at the end of it, which is what you asked for.

Doing it properly turned out to be more interesting than just changing a number, and it's raised a few things I'd rather ask you about than guess at. None of it is urgent. But it's the kind of thing that's cheap to decide now and expensive to change once people have got used to it.

**First, the good news about the house in Thailand.**

The way I built the hours already fits what you're describing, and by luck rather than foresight. The site doesn't have a schedule that someone has to fill in — every hour exists automatically, with a candle, whether or not anyone is leading it. Someone leading an hour is an *exception* that gets laid over the top.

Which means you don't need to cover all 8,760 hours in a year for this to work. You could have four hours a day led live and the other twenty carry on exactly as they do now, and nobody arriving at 3am sees a broken or empty site. That's worth knowing before you plan the rota, because "some hours are live" is a much smaller thing to commit to than "every hour is live", and the site can't tell the difference.

**Now the thing I keep coming back to.**

At the moment the site is very good at people *finishing* together and not good at all at people *starting* together.

When you arrive, the first thing it asks is how long you want to sit — which quietly frames the whole thing as your own private timer that other people happen to also be using. You can choose to finish on the shared bell, and if you do, you're told how many people began around the same time as you, and you can see their candles. But the shared part is something you have to opt into, at the bottom of the first question.

I don't think that's wrong. It was a deliberate choice, and it came from a good instinct — nobody should ever arrive wanting to meditate and be told to come back in forty minutes. But it does mean the feeling you're after, the one where you're sitting *with* someone, is the quieter of the two things the site offers rather than the main one.

The house in Thailand changes the arithmetic on that, because for the first time there'd be a reason to be there at a particular moment: a person is about to light a candle, and you can watch them do it. That's a much better argument for turning up on the hour than "other anonymous people might also be here".

So the question I'd like your answer to is really: **should joining the hour become the main way in, with sitting on your own straight away kept as the alternative?** Rather than the other way round, which is what it is today.

I'm not asking you to decide that on the spot. But it's the decision everything else hangs off, so it's worth us actually talking about rather than me picking one.

**Four smaller things, all downstream of that.**

**1. What happens in the five minutes?** Right now, nothing — it's just a gap where the shared bell doesn't ring. You can still start a sitting at :57, and the candle still burns the whole hour. But if someone is handing over to someone else, that's a real moment and it's currently invisible. Is that a changeover people *see*? One person saying goodnight and the next one settling in is quite a lovely thing to be allowed to watch, and it would be the clearest possible signal that a real human being is on the other end of this. Or is it a private staff matter that the site simply doesn't show? Both are defensible. They're very different builds.

**2. Who rings the bell?** At the moment the closing bell is a sound your own device makes. Under the video version, the bell at :55 could be one the person on screen actually strikes, with everyone hearing it at the same second. That's the single strongest version of "you meditated with someone" I can think of, and it makes your 55 a real number rather than an arbitrary one. It also means the live hours would feel meaningfully different from the ambient ones, which may or may not be what you want.

**3. Arriving just before the bell.** If you turn up between :50 and :55 and choose to sit with everyone, the site sends you to the *next* hour's bell — so you're looking at a sit of over an hour, or you give up and sit alone. It does this so you're not handed a two-minute sitting you didn't ask for, and I think that's right as far as it goes. But "come back in an hour" is exactly the thing we agreed the site should never say. The obvious alternative is somewhere to wait — a screen that says the next sitting starts in six minutes, with the candle and the people already gathering. That's close to the "waiting room" idea from your original brief, which we'd parked. Worth knowing whether you still want it, because this is the first thing that's genuinely asked for it.

**4. Does the hour get a shared beginning as well as a shared ending?** The strongest version is everyone starting together at :00 and finishing together at :55. The risk is that it brings back the thing we deliberately removed — someone arriving at :20 being told to wait. There are ways round it. But it's a real trade and I'd rather put it to you than quietly pick the safe option.

**Two things the house doesn't change, and I'd be doing you a disservice not to say so.**

Staffing was one of three reasons the live video was parked, and a household of people rotating genuinely does answer it. The other two are untouched.

**The streaming costs real money, every year, forever.** Not a one-off. It scales with how many people watch, which means it gets more expensive precisely when the site is going well. That needs a number attached to it before it's a plan.

**And putting identifiable people on camera in front of an anonymous audience is a safeguarding question, not a technical one.** It gets more serious, not less, if the people on camera are living at the address they're broadcasting from — which is what a shared house means. I'm not raising that to be difficult, and it's not a reason not to do it. It's a reason to talk to a solicitor before we build any of it, and I'd rather say so now than after you've invited people to move in.

**What I'd suggest.**

Nothing here blocks anything. The 55 minutes is done and the site is fine as it is. But question one — whether sitting *together* becomes the front door — is worth half an hour on the phone, and the other four mostly answer themselves once you've decided it.

Have a think and give me a ring whenever suits.
